### Lizard of Oz: Act One
> N1: Lizard of Oz is performed by: \[name each student and the role they are playing.\]
> N2: Our play begins with Bob Lizard looking for his wife Lizzie Lizard in order to find a safe place to hide from a bush fire.
> N3: Lizard of Oz: Act One
> N1: A clap of thunder broke the silence in the bush.
> N2: A flash of lightning hit the ground with a loud boom.
> N3: Smoke filled the air. All around the animals were very scared.
> N1: A fire had started in the bush and the animals needed to find a safe place to hide.
> N2: Bob Lizard was in a hurry and getting very worried.
> BL: Lizzie! Lizzie, where are you? Come out, come out, wherever you are. We have to get to the rocks. The fire is not far. Lizzie!
> N3: Shouted Bob as he searched for his wife Lizzie Lizard.
> N2: Lizzie didn't answer and was nowhere to be seen.
> BL: Lizzie! Lizzie! The fire is very close! We must find a safe place.
> N3: Bob continued to call for his wife but she did not respond.
> N1: Bob was beginning to get quite worried when he came upon Mrs Wombat preparing to hide from the impending fire.
> BL: Please Mrs Wombat, have you seen my wife?
> N2: Bob asked in a worried voice.
> BL: There's a bush fire coming and I need to know that she's all right.
> N3: Mrs Wombat's house was built in the ground. It was a long, narrow burrow with dirt heaped all over it. She would be safe there in a bush fire.
> MW: Lizzie didn't come this way.
> N1: Mrs Wombat replied to Bob.
> MW: In fact, I haven't seen her at all today. But if I do, I'll send her on. Don't worry; she knows where she belongs.
> N2: Mrs Wombat went on to say. Then she popped down into her burrow to stay safe from the impending bush fire.
> N3: Bob was moving along very quickly.
> N1: The smell of the bush fire was becoming quite strong now.
> N2: It was then that he came upon Mr Snake waiting outside of his hole at the base of the huge, old gum tree.
> BL: Please Mr Snake can you help me? I'm looking for my wife and I don't want her to get into any strife with the bush fire.
> N3: Bob pleaded with the snake.
> MS: SSSSSSorry …
> N1: Hissed Mr snake.
> MS: I haven't sssssseen Mrssssss Lizard. But if I do, you'll be the firsssst to know. I'll ssssend you a sssssignal.
> N2: And with that, Mr Snake promptly slithered back into his hole, safe from the bush fire.
> N3: By now, Bob was running out of time.
> N1: The bush fire was getting closer.
> N2: The smoke was getting thicker.
> N3: And the sky was getting darker and darker. He was becoming more and more concerned for Lizzie's safety.
> N1: As he got near a pile of rocks, he saw Brown Owl who had been woken up by all of the noise and the smell of smoke.
> BL: Brown Owl, I can't find my Lizzie and I'm beginning to feel dizzy with fear. Can you help me? She's never been away this long. I do hope there's nothing wrong.
> N2: Bob was becoming desperate with concern that something terrible had happened to Lizzie.
> BO: Don't worry my friend.
> N3: Hooted Brown Owl from the top of her perch in the tree.
> BO: With my big eyes I can see far and wide. I'll look for her as I fly by and let you know when I see her.
> BL: Thank you friend. I hope you will find her soon.
> N1: Bob was grateful as he watched Brown Owl lift off from the bough of her tree into the quickly darkening sky.
> N2: Brown Owl was not so sure though as she could see the bush fire raging towards them in the distance.
> BO: I think finding Lizzie in this terrible smoke will take a magic trick.
> N3: Thought Brown Owl to herself as she took off in search of her friend's beloved wife.
> N1: End of Act One.

### Lizard of Oz: Act Two
> N2: The smoke billowed in the hazy, grey sky.
> N3: Bob Lizard was becoming frantic with worry for his wife Lizzie.
> N1: The bush fire had been blazing for many hours and no one had seen Lizzie.
> N2: Just then, Bob saw Freda Fox.
> BL: Freda! Freda! I am so pleased to see you.
> N3: Gushed Bob Lizard hoping against all hope that the crafty fox would help him find Lizzie.
> BL: I need your help. Have you seen my wife Mrs Lizard? I'm very worried as the fire is becoming such a hazard.
> FF: I can't stop to help right now.
> N1: The cunning fox replied as she hardly even stopped to look at Bob.
> FF: I'm too cunning to stay here and melt in this heat. But if I see her along the way, I'll let her know that you are looking for her.
> N2: Lied the fox who had no intention of speaking to Mrs Lizard. The fox quickly scampered off into her den.
> BL: Oh dear! Lizzie! Lizzie, where are you?
> N3: Bob once again started calling hopelessly.
> N1: The ground was getting very hot as the flames were now very close. It was also becoming increasingly difficult to breathe.
> N2: Bob thought he might choke as he wriggled down into the riverbed to dig a hole under the rocks.
> N3: Suddenly, Bob stopped.
> N1: A pair of bright eyes was staring up at him.
> LL: What took you so long?
> N2: Lizzie said in a sweet, calm voice.
> LL: I've been here all day waiting for you. I was becoming quite concerned. I've laid these eggs.
> BL: Oh my lovely Lizzie, you're safe and sound. I've been calling for you everywhere.
> N3: Replied Bob.
> LL: I've been waiting here at the river for you to come. I smelt the smoke and heard the storm but I had to help our eggs stay warm. I'm so glad you are finally here because our babies will be hatched soon. We are going to be a larger family.
> N1: Bob and Lizzie waited patiently and soon the bush fire passed and the ground cooled down.
> N2: A sound was then heard from under the pile of rocks. Two speckled eggs cracked open and crawling out were two baby lizards.
> BL: Oh Lizzie they are the most beautiful children I've ever seen.
> N3: Bob was mesmerised by the little lizards as they searched for food immediately.
> LL: Yes Bob they are beautiful and they are our children. Today we are a bigger family.
> N1: End of Act Two

+ The end

## L38 | Oral Reading
focus: Oral reading

### Polly Parrot
A long time ago in a bush far away lived a peaceful, old wizard. Wizard was friends with all the bush animals and spoke with them every day.

One day his ~dear~ ~friend,~ Polly Parrot, came for a visit. Polly had ~dull,~ grey feathers and a little, pointy beak. She was a kind and good-natured bird.

In those days long ago, all of the animals were black, grey or brown and had no other colours at all. Polly didn't mind as she was a very ~humble~ ~and~ ~contented~ ~bird.~ Her friends, on the other hand, always tried to colour themselves with the juice from berries, without any success.

Wizard and Polly were chatting as they usually did, happily talking about the weather and the gum trees, when Wizard took out a piece of paper from his pocket.

"Oh, kind Polly," sighed Wizard to his friend. "I have to make a new potion today, but I do not know where to find some things. Will you please help me by searching for them?"

Polly happily agreed as she was always a helpful parrot. "I have written a list of colours and I need some fruit for each of them," said Wizard. So with the list of colours in her beak, Polly flew away in search of some green fruit.

Polly flew across the ~entire~ country. She flew high in the sky, and she whirled and twirled through the leaves of the trees and along the rivers, until she found a large patch of plants growing.

Down Polly flew, and landed in a ~vineyard.~ Polly grasped two bunches of big, juicy, green grapes in her claws and pulled and pulled. Finally, they came free. Feeling very proud, she flew back to Wizard.

"Thank you, thank you, thank you so much!" exclaimed Wizard. He tapped the grapes with his wand, which gave out a loud POP! Next, he dropped them into a bubbling ~cauldron.~ The water started to fizz and crackle, and then stopped. The cauldron let out a large puff of green smoke. Parrot was amazed.

"Wow! What is the potion for?" asked Polly.

"It makes the sun shine for a whole day," said Wizard. With that in mind, Polly took the list of colours and set off around the country again.

Polly flew over ~deserts,~ oceans and high mountains. She flew hundreds of miles until she spotted below a big ~orchard~ full of fruit trees. She could see lots and lots of juicy, round, orange-coloured balls. Polly swooped down and plucked two oranges from a tree, then flew back to Wizard with one in each claw.

"Oranges, you clever parrot," Wizard said as he tapped them like he did the grapes, and dropped them into the steaming cauldron. The potion fizzed and bubbled again, and this time gave off a large cloud of orange smoke.

Though Polly was feeling rather tired from all the flying, she took flight again and soon returned with the next fruit, which was yellow.

"What fruit is yellow?" asked Wizard. "Bananas of course," answered Polly. With that Polly went off and collected three more colourful ~pieces~ ~of~ ~fruit.~ She gathered some juicy strawberries, delicious blueberries and plump blackberries.

At last, after a very hard day's work, Polly had collected all the colours from Wizard's list.

"Thank you so much, Polly. You are a kind and helpful friend indeed," said Wizard.

"Because you have helped me so much today, I am going to reward you."

Wizard picked up his wand and gently tapped Polly three times on the head. Polly felt a funny tingle and buzz inside, and then it stopped. She looked ~cautiously~ down at her feathers. To her amazement, she saw that she was no longer dull grey. Polly's feathers were all the colours of the rainbow. She had red, orange, yellow, blue and purple feathers.

"I look like a rainbow in the sky!" shouted Polly with ~glee.~ "Oh, thank you so much Wizard. I never knew how bright and colourful I could be!"

It was not long before the other bush animals had heard the news and gathered around. "Where did you get such wonderful colours?" asked Raccoon.

"I did lots of hard work to earn them. I helped Wizard, so he rewarded me," replied Polly.

"I don't want to work hard!" shouted Raccoon. "I like having fun." And off he ~scampered~ to play.

The next morning, the sun rose slowly over the horizon and the warm rays of light touched Polly's brightly coloured feathers. She woke and stretched and looked around. There were hundreds of creatures around her, tiny and large, feathered and furred, young and old.

"We want to be helpful, but we don't know how. Can you show us?" twittered a little robin by her side. "Of course!" agreed Polly Parrot. So she showed all the creatures how to be kind, and how to help and look after each other.

When Wizard heard of this, he rewarded each animal — bird, insect and fish — that had become kind. But all the creatures that just ~messed~ ~around~ and had fun all day like Raccoon kept their black, grey and brown fur, and never got any colours.

All the kind animals carried on being helpful to each other, and still are today, with Polly the parrot as their role model. Because Polly was the most helpful to Wizard and worked the hardest without having to be asked, she will always have the brightest colours.

The little robin loved collecting strawberries and eventually changed his name to Robin Redbreast.

And that is how parrots got their amazing colours.

?qa Where did Wizard live?
= He lived in a bush far away.
?qa What was written on the piece of paper?
= Written on the piece of paper was a list of colours.
?qa Why was the smoke from the cauldron orange the second time Polly returned?
= Oranges had been dropped into the cauldron which caused the potion to give off orange smoke.
?qa Why did Polly say that she looked like a rainbow?
= Wizard had turned her dull grey feathers into all the bright colours of a rainbow.

### Chocolate Bananas
These chocolate bananas are fun and easy to make. They are great for birthday parties or just for a fun special treat. Children will love making these.

+ Ingredients:
- 6 ripe bananas
- 1 large bag of chocolate bits
- 1 cup of nuts \(crushed\)
- 1/2 cup of hundreds and thousands
- 12 satay cooking sticks
+ Method:
- 1. Peel the bananas.
- 2. Cut the bananas into halves and place a satay cooking stick into the cut end.
- 3. Place some cooking paper on a tray and lay the bananas out on the tray.
- 4. Put the chocolate bits in a bowl and melt them in a microwave stirring the mixture until it's smooth.
- 5. Dip the bananas into the chocolate.
- 6. Decorate the bananas with nuts and hundreds and thousands to taste.
- 7. If you want, you may place the bananas into the freezer until they are frozen.

### Luke and Joe at the Creek
Luke and Joe were playing on the rocks near the creek. The rocks sloped down the bank to the shallow water. The boys climbed down to the water's edge with their bucket and fishing nets. The creek was near their grandparents' house and was their favourite place to explore whenever they came to visit them.

The boys could see little fish darting in and out of the weeds in the muddy water. Luke jumped onto a big rock in the water. "Don't fall in, Luke," cried Joe. "Mum hasn't got any spare clothes in the car today. If you get wet, we'll have to go home straight away, and I'm having fun."

"You're right; I don't want to spoil the day," replied Luke. "Let's fill the bucket with water and see if we can catch some fish."

The boys played at the creek for a long time, throwing stones and skimming them across the water. They also caught lots of mud guppies. Then they heard their mum call them back for lunch. They tipped the guppies back into the creek and raced back up the rocky bank to their grandparents' house.

?qa Whom were Luke and Joe visiting?
= They were visiting their grandparents.
?qa Which word tells you that the water wasn't very deep?
= shallow
?qa Which word tells you that the water wasn't very clear?
= muddy
?qa What might cause the day to be spoilt for the boys?
= If they were to fall into the water, they would have to go home to change out of their wet clothes.
?qa What did the boys do when their mother called them for lunch?
= They tipped the guppies they had caught back into the creek and raced back up the rocky bank to their grandparents' house.
